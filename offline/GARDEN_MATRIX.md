# Garden auto-drive validation matrix (#80)

Generated by `offline/garden_matrix.py`, 2026-08-12.

## Method

Every catalog destination that has a park point is driven from **8 start
positions** spread across the map, and each run is repeated at **4 speed / turn
rate combinations** — (64, 32), (48, 20), (96, 48), (32, 24) units-per-frame and
angle-units-per-frame, i.e. turning radii from roughly 650 to 2600 units. A
(destination, start) pair counts as OK only if **all four** combinations arrive.

The Garden's true speed and turn rate are not known — the engine's probe table
(`0x53E7A0:0x53E84D`) says it marches `0x40` per step against foot's `0x20`, but
says nothing about rotation. Sweeping a 4x spread is the honest substitute for a
number we do not have, and it is a stronger test than fitting one: the route has
to be followable by a sluggish hull *and* by a nimble one.

The planner runs on the conservative **256-unit** grid; the simulated hull is
collided against the **128-unit** grid, which is finer. So the simulation is
genuinely testing whether a coarse route survives contact with real geometry,
not replaying its own assumptions.

Start positions: Balamb Garden's berth (24576, −29406); open ocean east
(60000, −45000); ocean south-west (−60000, 30000); inland Galbadia
(−40000, −30000); north sea (20000, −88000); central sea (0, 20000); far west
(−100000, −40000); south-east sea (70000, 60000).

## Result

**175 of 184 (destination, start) pairs pass — 95.1 %.** 736 individual
simulations. Most passing routes complete with **zero** replans and **zero**
reverse bursts.

16 of the 39 catalog destinations have no park point at all and are reported as
unreachable rather than driven — see the analysis document for why each one is
genuinely unreachable by the hull.

## The 9 failures

| destination | start | note |
|---|---|---|
| Dollet | south-east sea | final approach into the northern bay |
| Trabia Garden | Balamb berth | final approach |
| White SeeD Ship | south-east sea | final approach |
| Chocobo Forest 5 | south-east sea | long crossing, coastal berth |
| Chocobo Forest 7 | ocean SW, inland Galbadia, far west, SE sea | worst case: 4 of 8 |
| Fire Cavern | open ocean east | final approach |

All nine are **approach geometry**, not routing: the planner returns a valid
route every time and the hull covers the crossing, then cannot thread the last
few hundred units into a coastal berth from that particular heading. The failure
is safe and honest — the drive gives up after 12 replans, releases the keys and
says "Balamb Garden cannot get to X from here", leaving the player parked and in
control rather than grinding silently.

Two mitigations are in the shipping build:

* the arrival radius widens by 256 units per replan, but only onto ground that
  carries the disembark bit, so a slightly-off berth still counts as arrived;
* on final give-up, if the hull is standing on disembarkable ground within 6 km
  of the intended berth it parks there and announces the real remaining walk
  from where it actually stopped — a longer walk beats a dead stop.

## What was tried and rejected

Selecting park points with a clearance preference (charging 1.5 cells of detour
per cell of missing sea room) fixed 4 of the 9 failures — but it moved *every*
berth further out, roughly doubling the walk at White SeeD Ship (0 → 1280),
Winhill (768 → 1864) and Chocobo Forest 1 (768 → 1379). Trading a longer walk at
23 destinations for 4 fewer edge-case wedges is a bad deal for the player, so
the closest valid berth is what ships. The alternative table is reproducible by
setting the clearance weight in `garden_park.py`.

## Caveat

This is a simulation against a kinematic hull, not the engine. It cannot model
the Garden's real acceleration, its collision radius, or whatever the engine
does when a large object grazes a coast. The live BAT is the ground truth; the
matrix exists so that the *logic* — routing, waypoint pursuit, wall-following,
stall recovery — is known not to wedge itself before it ever runs in the game.
